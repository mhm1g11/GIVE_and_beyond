# International methane emissions

## Scope and boundaries

Account for methane emissions attributable to fossil fuel imports using supplier geography and fuel type. Keep natural gas transported by pipeline distinct from LNG, and document which LNG segments, such as liquefaction, are included under each methodological boundary. The emission boundary used in a calculation must match the denominator used for an intensity standard.

## Fuel-specific methods to document

### Natural gas and LNG

Record partner-country imports, physical origin attribution where transit countries differ from production origin, methane intensities, upstream coverage, and any LNG-specific additions. Separate production, processing, transmission, liquefaction, shipping and regasification where applicable. Avoid applying one upstream threshold indiscriminately to all supply-chain stages.

### Crude oil

Record imported quantities, producer origin, upstream methane, oil/gas production allocation and the precise intensity denominator (mass, energy or volume equivalent). Trace how venting and flaring are treated.

### Coal

Distinguish methane emitted by production from coal-mine emissions at abandoned assets. Explain whether the regulatory mechanism is an intensity counterfactual, technology-specific mitigation, or another explicit assumption.

## Scenario construction

For each combination of fuel, origin and technology, preserve the baseline emissions, the policy counterfactual and the assumptions that translate intensity changes into annual tonnes of CH₄. Provide sensitivity cases for uncertain origins, intensities and compliance.
