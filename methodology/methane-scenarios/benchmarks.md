# Benchmarks and validation

External studies provide checks on boundaries, magnitudes and assumptions, but their estimates must be normalised for energy mix, year, fuel coverage and intensity definition before numerical comparison.

## Comparison register

| Benchmark | What to compare | Key comparability question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Climate Analytics (2026), methane import standards | Imported oil, gas and coal mitigation | Are the LNG boundary and intensity denominator consistent? |
| Öko-Institut (2026), EU import regulation | Oil and gas reductions under alternative thresholds | Are source years and units identical? |
| IEA Global Methane Tracker | Producer-country and technology methane | Are satellite and abandoned-asset categories handled consistently? |
| DBI/BMWK (2025), German gas supply | German origin mix and supply-chain emissions | Is physical origin reconstructed rather than merely transit country? |
| GEM abandoned coal mine data | Coal emissions from retired mines | Are mine coverage, flood status and vintage aligned? |

Record any divergence as a source of methodological information rather than adjusting assumptions simply to fit a headline result. Add numerical benchmark comparisons only alongside the exact run, benchmark definition and conversion procedure.

External source documents may carry distinct reuse conditions and should be cited or linked, not republished wholesale.
